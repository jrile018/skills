# Learning, Assignments, and Projects

## Module contract

- **Purpose:** Build prerequisite-aware study plans, diagnose misconceptions, and guide assignments or projects without overstating source coverage.
- **Load when:** The user requests tutoring, sequencing, hints, review, exercises, reproduction, or project design.
- **Do not load when:** The user wants a direct professional calculation with no teaching component.
- **Inputs:** Target capability, current attempt, background, available time/tools, desired disclosure level, and assessment constraints.
- **Output invariant:** Match the intervention to the learner and finish with a transfer or verification task.

## Tutoring workflow

1. Identify the target capability and the learner's current attempt.
2. Find the earliest missing prerequisite or invalid inference.
3. Give the least revealing useful intervention: diagnostic question, assumption check, counterexample, hint, outline, then full derivation when appropriate.
4. Ask the learner to apply the method to a changed example or adversarial edge case.
5. Distinguish mathematical misunderstanding, notation confusion, computational error, and unsupported financial interpretation.

Respect academic-integrity and user-specified help levels. The source corpus maps assignments and cases; it is not a solution manual.

## Dependency-based study route

1. Contracts, cash flows, bond mathematics, matrices, and probability.
2. Regression, inference, time series, volatility, factors, and PCA.
3. Portfolio choice, constraints, risk measures, and calibration stability.
4. Markov processes, martingales, Brownian motion, Itō calculus, and SDEs.
5. Replication, risk-neutral valuation, numerical pricing, rates, commodities, and credit.
6. A reproducible project with explicit assumptions, dated data, baselines, out-of-sample checks, and limitations.

## Assignment and case-study map

The source corpus catalogs nine 2013 problem sets, five 2024 problem sets, six substantial 2013 case studies, group/final-paper work, and an investment observation exercise. Use them to identify competencies and task shapes; do not use source problems or close paraphrases as held-out evaluation.

Strong project families include:

- factor/regression and volatility diagnostics;
- equal-weight versus constrained or minimum-variance sensitivity;
- binomial, Monte Carlo, and closed-form option valuation comparison;
- yield-curve bootstrapping and PCA stability;
- close-to-close versus range-based volatility estimation.

Every empirical project should preserve observation dates, source universe, transformations, contemporaneous information, baseline, and uncertainty.

## Misconception checks

Use the parent invariants as misconception targets. In learning work, also check whether the learner is treating a project or lecture title as complete instruction, confusing a named method with evidence that its assumptions hold, or reproducing steps without transfer to changed conditions.

## Evidence of learning

Use changed assumptions, a new surface form, a counterexample, or a method comparison. Grade correctness, assumptions, method choice, verification, interpretation, and calibrated uncertainty separately.
