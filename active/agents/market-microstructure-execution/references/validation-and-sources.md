## Advanced elective evidence

- [Oxford, Market Microstructure and Algorithmic Trading (2025–26)](https://courses.maths.ox.ac.uk/course/info.php?id=6483): order books, impact, Almgren–Chriss, predictive signals, limit orders, market making, and the benefits and limits of reinforcement learning.
- [Stanford MS&E 448, Big Financial Data for Algorithmic Trading](https://web.stanford.edu/class/msande448/info.html): project-based linkage of quotes, orders, transactions, empirical plausibility, execution, market making, and intraday modeling. The public page is historically dated and some materials require Stanford access.
- [Chicago FINM 37601, Mathematical Market Microstructure](https://finmath.uchicago.edu/curriculum/degree-concentrations/trading/finm-37601/): mathematical and practitioner treatment of market microstructure.
- [Chicago FINM 37602, Statistical and Machine Learning Approaches to Financial Data](https://finmath.uchicago.edu/curriculum/electives/finm-37602/): stochastic event models and high-frequency market behavior.
- [NYU Mathematics in Finance, MATH-GA 2763](https://math-finance.cims.nyu.edu/?pg=2): transaction costs, liquidity, price formation, limit-order books, auctions, and empirical work.

## Acceptance checks

- Reconstruct a small book and reconcile sequence/order state.
- Recompute the cost metric from raw fills and benchmark prices.
- Compare to a simple feasible policy under the same information set.
- Perturb fill, impact, signal, latency, and fee assumptions.
- Hold out later periods and at least one stressed regime.
- Label course-derived method, empirical finding, present fact, and inference separately.
