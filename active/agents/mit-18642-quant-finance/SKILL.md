# MIT 18.642 Quantitative Finance

Use the public Fall 2024 corpus to connect a finance question to the right mathematical object, support learning, and verify results without overstating course coverage.

## Route the Request

| Request condition | Course block |
|---|---|
| Terms, bonds, discounting, one-period models, or rates | Financial foundations |
| Regression, hypothesis testing, PCA, time series, volatility, or ML | Empirical methods |
| Portfolio construction or counterparty-risk optimization | Portfolio and risk |
| Stochastic processes, risk-neutral valuation, Black–Scholes, calculus, or SDEs | Stochastic pricing |
| Prerequisites, lecture coverage, problem sets, group project, or final paper | Learning and provenance |

Choose the smallest block set that owns distinct decisions. Order blocks only when one produces an input required by another, such as an estimated covariance feeding portfolio construction.

Read [references/course-guide.md](references/course-guide.md) for the detailed map, learning workflow, verification checks, public-material limits, and evaluation cases.

## Work

1. Identify the learner's or analyst's objective, supplied work, prerequisites, information set, and desired help level.
2. Translate the financial question into a mathematical object before choosing a formula.
3. State assumptions and preserve physical-versus-pricing, estimate-versus-decision, and historical-versus-current distinctions.
4. Derive, compute, or give the smallest useful hint. For empirical work, use time-ordered evaluation and point-in-time inputs.
5. Verify with dimensions, limiting cases, covariance properties, replication, simulation, diagnostics, or a second derivation.
6. Label content as public-course-grounded, standard extension, fresh external evidence, or unavailable.

## Boundaries

Lecture 22 on systematic trading is listed in the calendar but unavailable to OCW learners. Do not reconstruct, summarize, or attribute detailed methods to it.

Use 15.450 for an advanced multi-method workflow centered on econometrics, dynamic optimization, stochastic pricing, or Monte Carlo. Use 15.481x for market behavior and hedge-fund ecology. Use 6.172 for measured code performance. Do not infer live profitability, recommend securities, execute trades, or mutate production systems.
