# Validation and Source Map

## Loading boundary

Read this module for empirical validation, stress design, attribution, curriculum provenance, or maintenance of this skill. Do not load it for every portfolio calculation.

## Evaluation contract

Before comparing portfolios, freeze:

- the rebalance clock and information cutoff;
- point-in-time universe and data revisions;
- estimator windows and hyperparameter selection procedure;
- benchmark and simple baselines;
- leverage, financing, liquidity, turnover, and cost rules;
- walk-forward, nested-validation, or untouched-test boundaries;
- metrics for return, drawdown, tail behavior, turnover, concentration, and capacity.

Use block or time-aware uncertainty procedures when dependence matters. Attribute gains to forecasts, risk estimates, constraints, costs, and timing through ablations. Report the number of strategies or specifications tried when multiple testing could make the winner misleading.

## Source-backed curriculum spine

- [NYU Courant Active Portfolio Management](https://math-finance.cims.nyu.edu/?pg=2) motivates advanced construction beyond mean-variance, including robust, dynamic-programming, and Bayesian approaches, plus econometric estimation issues.
- [Chicago FINM 36700 Portfolio Theory and Risk Management II](https://finmath.uchicago.edu/curriculum/required-courses/finm-36700/) connects mean-variance analysis, factors, forecasting, tail risk, cross-asset allocation, model selection, and implementation with real data.
- [Columbia MSFE curriculum](https://ieor.columbia.edu/msfe-curriculum) places asset allocation, quantitative risk, stochastic control, and computational finance in an advanced financial-engineering sequence.
- [Oxford MSc in Mathematical and Computational Finance course list](https://courses.maths.ox.ac.uk/mod/book/view.php?chapterid=816&id=65619) supplies stochastic control, risk, computational finance, volatility, asset-pricing, and microstructure context.
- [MIT 15.433 Investments lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-433-investments-spring-2003/pages/lecture-notes/) provide historical open course material on portfolio theory, factor pricing, active management, risk, and hedge funds.
- [MIT 15.450 Analytics of Finance](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/15-450-analytics-of-finance-fall-2010/pages/syllabus/) remains a separate course skill for integrated econometrics, pricing, Monte Carlo, volatility, and dynamic choice.

Course pages establish topics and instructional scope; they do not validate a trading strategy or make historical examples current. Refresh time-sensitive claims from primary market, regulatory, or data sources.
