# Claim ledger and verification guide

Use a claim ledger for research with multiple consequential claims. It may remain internal unless the user asks to see it.

| Claim | Importance | Source | What the source establishes | Scope/date | Confidence | Conflict or gap |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---|
| Concise proposition | High/medium/low | Direct link or local location | Exact supporting result, rule, or statement | Applicable population, version, and date | High/medium/low | Contradiction, missing detail, or none |

## Source checks

For every high-importance claim, ask:

1. Is the source primary or authoritative for this exact claim?
2. Does it state the claim directly, or am I inferring it?
3. Is the source current enough for the question?
4. Does its population, jurisdiction, product version, or experimental setup match?
5. Is a caveat, denominator, baseline, or negative result being omitted?
6. Is there independent evidence or a credible disagreement worth representing?

## Gap-driven searching

Search again only for a named gap: missing primary evidence, unresolved conflict, unclear date, absent denominator, or an option that may have been omitted. Avoid accumulating repetitive articles that all repeat the same upstream source.

## Stop conditions

Research is ready to synthesize when:

- every decision-changing claim has adequate support or is labeled unknown;
- material disagreements are explained rather than averaged away;
- major alternatives have been checked for omission;
- citations point to pages that were actually inspected;
- the remaining uncertainty would not change the recommendation, or is explicitly presented to the user.
