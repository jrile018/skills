# Grounded Research Workflow

Produce a useful synthesis whose important claims can be traced to evidence. Treat model knowledge as orientation, not a source.

## Frame the research

Identify the decision or question, relevant date, scope, jurisdiction or population, acceptable source types, and consequence of error. If the user has not specified a research depth, choose the least expensive level that can answer responsibly:

- **Quick lookup:** one narrow fact with an authoritative source.
- **Comparative research:** several options, claims, or sources.
- **Deep research:** a contested or multi-part question requiring iterative search, synthesis, and gap checking.

Browse whenever the answer depends on current or unstable information. For technical claims, prefer official documentation and original research. For laws, policies, statistics, pricing, and product behavior, prefer the responsible institution or vendor. Use secondary sources for context and independent criticism, not as automatic substitutes for primary evidence.

## Build evidence before prose

Read [references/claim-ledger.md](references/claim-ledger.md) for comparative or deep research, or whenever several material claims must be supported. Record what each source actually establishes, not merely that it discusses the topic.

Separate:

- verified source facts;
- reasonable inferences from those facts;
- source disagreement;
- missing evidence and unresolved uncertainty.

Citations are navigation aids, not proof. Open decisive sources, check dates and scope, and make sure the cited page supports the nearby claim. Agreement among several LLMs does not count as corroboration.

## Stop responsibly

Stop when the material claims are supported at the required confidence, important conflicts are represented, and another search round is unlikely to change the decision. Do not hide gaps behind polished prose.

For high-stakes conclusions, state the limits of the research and recommend qualified review where appropriate. Do not transmit private material to external search or analysis services without authorization.

## Report

Lead with the answer or decision-relevant conclusion. Then give the strongest evidence, meaningful counterevidence, conflicts, and unknowns. Place citations beside supported claims and clearly label inference. Include methodology only to the extent that it helps the user judge completeness or reproduce the result.
